# What's Next

You've spent 3 hours with Wordflow. Here's where to go from here, in order of effort.

---

## In the next 24 hours (5 minutes)

- **Open the post-workshop email.** It has links to today's snapshots, the Session-2 workspace archive, and the docs. (The desktop install link lives on **sih.tools/wordflow** — the same URL you used to launch today.)
- **Save the workshop files** to your own machine if you want them long-term.

---

## In the next week (1–2 hours)

- **Read the tutorial.** The Wordflow documentation has a step-by-step walkthrough for each tool. Start with the [Index page](https://atap.github.io/ldaca-wordflow-docs/v0.5/tutorials/) and pick the tools you saw today.
- **Try one analysis on your own data.** Even a small experimental CSV. The point is to make Wordflow part of your toolkit, not just a workshop memory.
- **Save your workspace as an archive.** Snapshots are great for individual results; the workspace archive (Data Loader → Export workspace archive) backs up your whole project as one portable file.

---

## In the next month (a real project)

- **Pick one research question you'd otherwise answer by reading.** Try answering it with concordance, frequency, or topic modelling first.
- **Combine close and distant reading.** Wordflow doesn't replace careful reading — it surfaces patterns that *guide* careful reading. Use it to find the 20 documents worth reading in your corpus of 2,000.
- **Share a snapshot with a colleague.** The recipient opens the snapshot in their own Wordflow and sees exactly what you saw — no re-running.
- **Or share a workspace archive.** When you want them to *continue* your analysis (not just see it), send the archive. They get all the data blocks and can branch from any point.

---

## When you get stuck

- **In-app `?` icons** — fastest answer for "what does this control do?"
- **Feedback button (bottom of the left sidebar, next to Tutorial)** — the developers read every one. "This is confusing" is useful feedback.
- **Documentation site** — fastest answer for "how do I do X?"
- **GitHub issues** — best place to report bugs. The maintainers actually read them.
- **LDaCA / ATAP support channels** — links in the post-workshop email.
- **Facilitator email** — for follow-up questions specific to your research project.

---

## Want to install Wordflow on your own machine?

Three options, easiest first:

1. **Use the cloud (Binder) version.** Same URL the workshop used. Free, no install. Limitation: data is uploaded to the server during your session and deleted when the session ends — don't use it for sensitive or unpublished work.

2. **Desktop app (Mac / Windows).** A native installer that runs everything on your own machine. Slower to start the first time, faster after. **Recommended for serious research work.** Link in the email.

3. **Python install (`pip install ldaca-wordflow`).** For Python users who want CLI access. Requires Python 3.14+ and ~500 MB of disk.
