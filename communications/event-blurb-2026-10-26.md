## Long version (Eventbrite description, web page)

**Title:** Text analytics without programming: an introduction to LDaCA Wordflow

**Summary (short field, under 140 characters):**
A free hands-on introduction to Wordflow, a point-and-click text analysis app for researchers. Bring your laptop; no programming needed.

**Description:**

Do you work with text (interviews, social media, news, speeches, archives, survey comments) but not with Python or R? **LDaCA Wordflow** is a free text analysis app built for exactly that: word frequency, concordance, trends over time, topic modelling and, new this year, **GenAI-assisted annotation**, all point-and-click, with your data visible at every step.

This **in-person, ⟨2.5/3⟩-hour workshop** at the University of Melbourne, co-hosted by the **Melbourne Data Analytics Platform (MDAP)** and the **Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA)**, is an introduction for people who have never used it.

**What you'll do**

- **Learn how Wordflow thinks.** Text data flows through small, single-purpose tools you stack together; the meaning of an analysis comes from how you shape your data, not from the tool itself. Once this clicks, the whole app makes sense.
- **Follow a real research workflow, built live.** From a raw sample dataset to a small finding: preparing and joining data, comparing word use between groups, reading words in context, tracing change over time, and finding topics, then moving results from one tool into the next.
- **Try GenAI text coding the researcher-first way.** With the **Annotation** tool, you write a codebook, code a small sample yourself, let an AI model code the same sample, and check its agreement with you before trusting it with more. The AI is treated as one more coder that has to earn your trust.
- **Take it home.** The materials (step sheets and ready-made workspaces) are yours to keep, so you can repeat everything on your own data afterwards.

**Who it's for:** researchers at any stage, from HDR students to senior academics, in any field where text is data. No programming experience needed; that is the point. Open to researchers from all disciplines and universities.

**What to bring:** your own laptop (Mac or Windows). A short email a few days beforehand will ask you to install the free desktop app (about five minutes) and give you everything else you need. If you can't install software, a browser option will be available.

**When and where:** Monday 26 October 2026, ⟨start⟩–⟨end⟩. ⟨Room, building⟩, University of Melbourne, Parkville.

Wordflow is developed at the University of Sydney (Sydney Informatics Hub and Sydney Corpus Lab) as part of the **Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA)**, an ARDC co-investment partnership enabled by NCRIS. Screenshots, installers and materials from past workshops are at [sih.tools/wordflow](https://sih.tools/wordflow).
