Free hands-on intro to LDaCA Wordflow: word frequencies to topic models and GenAI-assisted coding, point and click. Bring your laptop.

Description

Do you work with text (interviews, social media, news, speeches, archives, survey comments) but not with Python or R? LDaCA Wordflow is a free text analysis app built for exactly that: word frequency, concordance, trends over time, topic modelling and, new this year, GenAI-assisted annotation, all point-and-click, with your data visible at every step.

This in-person, 3-hour workshop at the University of Melbourne, co-hosted by the Melbourne Data Analytics Platform (MDAP), the Language Data Commons of Australia (LDaCA) and the Sydney Informatics Hub, is an introduction for people who have never used it.

What you'll do

  • Get to know the app: how your data moves between its tools, and how to shape it for the question you're asking.
  • Follow a real research workflow, built live: from a raw dataset to a small finding, comparing word use between groups, reading words in context, tracing change over time and finding topics.
  • Try GenAI text coding the researcher-first way: with the Annotation tool, write a codebook, code a small sample yourself, and check how well an AI model agrees with you before letting it code more.

Who it's for: researchers at any stage, from HDR students to senior academics, in any field where text is data. No programming experience needed. Open to researchers from all disciplines and universities.

What to bring: your own laptop (Mac or Windows). A short email a few days beforehand will ask you to install the free desktop app (about five minutes). If you can't install software, a browser option is available.

When and where: Monday 26 October 2026, ⟨start⟩–⟨end⟩. ⟨Venue⟩, University of Melbourne.
