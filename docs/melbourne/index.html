Before we start

Each part starts with a few minutes on screen, then it's your turn.

  • Try it is the main steps: the same for everyone, and the only ones we expect you to finish. Take your time; helpers come to you.
  • If you finish early: a few ideas to try, in any order. They're suggestions, not tasks.

Each step shows the tool (in red), what to do, why, and the result to check for. Round boxes are Data Blocks: the data a part starts from. Names in bold are the exact words on Wordflow's buttons and menus. Fall behind at any point? Load the part's checkpoint and carry on.

  1. Open Wordflow and accept the update it offers. No prompt? Help → Check for Updates. Not installed? sih.tools/wordflow: the desktop app for Mac or Windows, or Launch in Binder to run it in your browser.
  2. Keep this page open beside Wordflow.
  3. Stuck? Put your hand up: a helper will come to you.

Lightbulb suggestions may pop up as you work: they appear when a result looks off and point at the setting that may help. Read them, or click Dismiss.

    Data Loader · Add

    Files into Data Blocks

    Importing brings in files, not yet data. In the file list, open sample_data → ADO → twitter. Click Add beside qldelection2020_candidate_tweets.csv, then Add to Project. Do the same for candidate_info_gender.csv.

    Then sample_data → SCL: Add beside Honi_Soit.zip → Texts as one Data Block (100) → Add to Project: 100 news articles from the University of Sydney's student newspaper, one row each, for Topic Modelling before the break.

    Why

    Result

  3. 3
    Data Editor · Overview, column type

    Look, then fix one thing

    Click the tweets block: the table under the graph is the Data Editor. Click Overview for a summary. The column created_at holds dates written as text: click the small type symbol at the left of its header, choose Date and time, then Convert.

    Why

    Result

  4. 4
    Data Builder · Join

    Join tweets to candidates

    Open Data Builder in the sidebar, then Join. Add Data Block: the tweets first (the first one is the left table), then the candidates. Set Left column and Right column to username. New Data Block name: Tweets. Click Add to Project.

    Why
    tweets carry no party or gender; the candidates table does
    Result
    Tweets: 2,380 rows, each with party and gender
  5. 5
    Data Builder · Filter

    Drop the retweets

    The Filter tab. Add Data Block Tweets, Add condition: text, starts with, RT, and tick negate (keep the tweets that do not start with RT). New Data Block name: Tweets_Original. Add to Project.

    Why
    count what candidates wrote, not what they passed on
    Result
    Tweets_Original: 1,646 tweets
  6. 6
    Frequency

    A first look at the words

    Open Frequency in the sidebar. Add Data Block Tweets_Original, Text column text, and under Tokeniser model pick the one under Recommended. Run. Among the biggest words: https, co and t. Nobody wrote them: they're pieces of web links (https://t.co/…).

    Why
    see what dominates before you analyse
    Result
    https, co, t near the top: link pieces in 1,153 tweets
  7. 7
    Data Editor · Clean text

    Clean them out

    Click Tweets_Original in the Project Graph. In the Data Editor, Clean text → Remove web links (URLs). Column: text; Save result to: The same column. The preview shows the links gone: Apply. Back in Frequency, Run again. Changed your mind? Undo in the Data Editor.

    Why
    links aren't words anyone wrote
    Result
    https, co, t are gone from the cloud

Look at the Project Graph: every step you took is a block, and the arrows are your method.

Part 3 · Topics 3:35

Three things here are new since August: reading a topic's texts, naming topics, and colouring the map by your own columns.

Wordflow reads each text in passages: a sentence, a paragraph, or a few paragraphs together, up to the Max tokens you set. A tweet is a single passage. A news article is several, and each passage can belong to a different topic.

No Topic Modelling runs to look at? Load MDAP-Part3: both runs are in it, finished, with some topics already named.

Try it

Tweets run
  1. 1
    Topic Modelling · the map

    The topic map

    Each bubble is a topic, sized by how many tweets it holds. Hover a bubble to see its words. Grey Ungrouped holds the texts that fit no topic.

    Why
    topics at a glance
    Result
    topics sized by tweets; Ungrouped isn't forced into one
  2. 2
    Topic Modelling · Show examples, Edit stop words

    Read the texts, not just the words

    In the topic list, click the eye (Show examples). Most typical shows the passages closest to the heart of the topic first. Open document shows the whole tweet a passage comes from.

    Words in the topic's list that tell you nothing (handles, names, amp)? In Display options, turn the stop words switch on, click the pencil (Edit stop words), add them, Save. They leave the word lists; the topics stay the same, so there's no need to run again.

    Why
    a topic is its texts, not its words
    Result
    you can say what a topic is about
  3. 3
    Topic Modelling · Rename

    Name a topic

    Name a topic you can now describe: the pencil (Rename), or double-click the topic's title. The name follows the topic everywhere.

    Why
    keep your reading with the topic
    Result
    the name, wherever the topic appears
  4. 4
    Topic Modelling · Display options

    Colour by party

    Colour by party (in Display options, beside or below the map). Labor wrote three tweets in four, so look for the bubbles that aren't Labor's.

    Why

Part 4 · Coding text with GenAI 4:00

An AI model is just another coder. It earns trust the way a human coder does: a codebook, a pilot, agreement scores, and revision. We code by hand first, then check the model against ourselves and against labels our team has checked.

Everyone starts this part from MDAP-Part4, so the time goes on coding rather than setup. It holds the 226 tweets that mention jobs, the labels our team checked for them (theme.reference, also on its own: tweets_job_reference.csv), and this codebook:

Task context: Tweets posted by candidates around the 2020 Queensland state election (polling day 31 October 2020). Retweets are left out, and only tweets containing "job" or "jobs" are included: 226 tweets. We are studying how candidates talked about employment: as a promise of jobs created or protected, or as a warning about jobs cut or lost. Code each tweet's main message about jobs, not passing mentions. Campaign slogans and lists of values ("For jobs. For health.") count as the candidate's own commitment. If a tweet both promises jobs and attacks cuts, choose Promise unless the cuts are spelled out as its main message. Cutting prices, costs or taxes is not cutting jobs. If "job" is not about employment ("did a great job"), or the tweet has no real jobs message, choose Other.
CodeDescription
PromiseThe tweet's main message is jobs being created, protected or supported: announcements, funding, infrastructure or training plans, or claims of jobs already delivered.
CutsThe tweet's main message is jobs being cut, lost or at risk: past sackings, warnings that a party will cut jobs, or attacks on an opponent's cuts.
OtherThe word job is used another way: praise like 'did a great job', commentary about job statistics, or anything that fits neither class above.

The Task context tells the model, and any human coder, what the texts are, why we are coding them, and how to decide the hard cases. It is saved with the codebook, so it travels with the Project and belongs in your methods section.

A codebook to try

Sentiment toward the LNP, on the same 226 tweets. Download Codebook_LNP_sentiment.csv: Add it in the Data Loader, then in Annotation, under Codebook, Add Data Block → this block (Code column class, Description column description). Or type it in: Codebook → Create new → Edit, paste the Task context, Add code for each code, Save. Code a page by hand before the model does.

Task context: Tweets posted by candidates around the 2020 Queensland state election that mention jobs. The Liberal National Party (LNP), led by Deb Frecklington, was in opposition. We want to know how candidates positioned themselves against the LNP. Judge the sentiment toward the LNP or its leader specifically, not the tweet's overall tone. If neither is mentioned or referred to, choose none.
CodeDescription
negCriticises, attacks or blames the LNP or its leader.
posPraises or supports the LNP or its leader.
neuMentions them without a clear judgement.
noneDoesn't mention or refer to the LNP or its leader.

Before you use this in your own research

  • The workshop key is deleted at 5:00 pm today. For your research, use your own key (the steps are the same), or a local model: in Add provider, choose Custom and point it at a local server such as Ollama or LM Studio, so your data never leaves your computer.
  • Check your ethics approval and your institution's rules on AI use: they decide which models you may use and whether your data may be sent to an external service. De-identify regardless. You remain responsible for everything the AI does on your behalf.
  • Keep your method on record: codebook, task context, model, examples and agreement scores belong in your methods section.
